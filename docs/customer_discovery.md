## Questions to test

1. When do renters research a building: before scheduling, before applying, or before signing?
2. Which records matter most: heat, pests, noise, maintenance violations, or something else?
3. Can a renter distinguish a 311 complaint, a verified HPD violation, and an owner-reported filing after a short explanation?
4. Does a source-linked report change the questions a renter plans to ask?
5. Would the renter compare two or three addresses, download a report, or share it with a roommate?
6. What wording feels useful without implying that the product certifies safety?

## Interview script

Target five to eight 15-minute interviews with recent renters, students planning a move, or tenant advocates.

1. Ask the participant to describe their latest apartment search and where they felt uncertain.
2. Show the sample HTML report without explaining it first. Ask them to narrate what they think each number means.
3. Ask which section they would use and which they would ignore.
4. Test comprehension: “Does an open 311 complaint mean the condition was verified?” and “Does this BBL necessarily identify one entrance?”
5. Ask the participant to choose one next action: inspect, ask the broker, request documents, compare another address, or stop researching.
6. End with: “What would make this report more trustworthy or more useful?”

Avoid leading questions such as “Would this useful product make you feel safer?” Do not collect apartment numbers, immigration status, income, or other sensitive personal information unless the research protocol genuinely requires it.

## Decision rules

- Prioritize a feature only after the same need appears in at least three interviews or one domain expert identifies a material legal or safety issue.
- Change copy immediately when two participants interpret a complaint as a verified condition.
- Keep multi-address comparison in scope if at least half of renter participants say they actively compare finalists.
- Pause the follow-up label if participants read it as a property grade despite the disclaimer.

## Midterm update format
